# Meeting notes, Fri 16 Oct 2026: Daniel's flavin-tryptophan pair

Figures are in `figures/` (made by `figures.py`). Every number below comes from a recorded run in `results/`.

## What was done (15-16 Oct)

- Put in Daniel's real molecule (`flavin_tryptophan_hypothesis` in `model.py`): two nitrogens (FN5 on electron 1,
  WN1 on electron 2), the dipolar pull between the electrons (EED), no exchange, 50 µT field. Two pairs:
  - **A, classical:** singlet start, k_r = k_f = 1 per µs.
  - **B, CISS:** χ = π/2 (maximal), k_r = 10, k_f = 1 per µs. Starts in cos(χ/2)|S⟩ + sin(χ/2)|T0⟩ and recombines
    through cos(χ/2)|S⟩ − sin(χ/2)|T0⟩, as Daniel specified.
- New way to point the field in any direction inside the molecule (`field_direction_hypothesis`). The old turning code
  only covered one ring of directions for a molecule with no symmetry axis (old results unaffected; `IDEAS.md` 21).
- **Contrast**, Daniel's measure: highest minus lowest recombination yield over a set of field directions, i.e. how
  good a compass the pair is (`recombination_contrast` in `engine.py`). Optimiser score: minus |C_A − C_B|.
- Engine checked at the new size (36 dimensions) against the slow reference: agrees to about 1e-7.

## Main findings

- **The CISS pair is the better compass.** No drive: contrast 0.0206 (CISS) against 0.0054 (classical), about 4×.
- **Daniel's FN5 shortcut half-holds.** The best and worst field directions are 22-39° from FN5's axes, not on them.
  FN5 axes alone capture 82% (classical) and 60% (CISS) of the full contrast. The two pairs' best and worst
  directions also differ, so the contrast uses all four.
- **Even a weak drive (0.35 MHz) matters, at specific frequencies.** One tone at 32.5 MHz cuts the classical
  contrast by about 70%; 30 MHz roughly doubles it. Sharp resonances.
- **Optimised drive: +35%** in |C_A − C_B| (0.0152 → 0.0206), against +25% (0.0190) for the best single tone. It
  works both ways: CISS compass stronger (0.0206 → 0.0250), classical weaker (0.0054 → 0.0044).
- **The best drive collapsed to one tone** (about 3.98 MHz at the cap; another search ended on 28.5 MHz alone).
  That is a result, as in the earlier work. It beats the tone scan partly because the optimiser also tunes the phase.

## Figure 1: `field_map.png`, yield against field direction (no drive)

- Two maps: classical pair (left), CISS pair (right). Each point is one direction of the Earth's field, inside the
  molecule. Read it like a world map: **up-down (θ)** is latitude (0° at the top = field along the molecule's z axis,
  180° = the opposite way); **left-right (φ)** is longitude around that axis.
- **Colour:** recombination yield (fraction of pairs ending as the recombination product). Dark = more, light =
  less. Each map has its own scale (CISS yields are higher overall, about 0.44 against 0.28).
- **Black ▼ / ▲:** directions of lowest / highest yield. **Numbered circles:** FN5's three axes, each both ways.
- **How to read it:** the more the colour varies, the better the compass; the contrast in each title is highest
  minus lowest.
- **Takeaways:** CISS contrast about 4× classical (0.0206 against 0.0054). ▼ and ▲ are not on the FN5 circles, so the
  shortcut is approximate. The left map repeats itself on opposite sides (reversing the field gives the same yield
  for the classical pair); the CISS map does not, because CISS gives the pair a built-in spin direction.

## Figure 2: `tone_contrast.png`, what a single radio wave does

- For each frequency (x-axis, 0.5-60 MHz), both pairs are driven with one sine wave at the fixed strength
  (0.35 MHz), and each pair's contrast is recomputed.
- **Top panel:** each pair's contrast against drive frequency (classical blue, CISS orange). Dashed lines = no drive.
  Above the line: the drive strengthened that compass; below: weakened it.
- **Bottom panel:** |C_A − C_B|, how well the drive helps tell the pairs apart. Grey dashed = no drive (0.0152).
- **Takeaways:** effects only at specific frequencies (sharp dips and spikes = resonances). 32.5 MHz cuts the
  classical contrast by about 70%; 30 MHz roughly doubles it. Best single tone 40.5 MHz: 0.0190, about +25%.
  Jagged partly because frequencies are 0.5 MHz apart, so narrow resonances may be under-sampled.

## Figure 3: `overnight_contrast.png`, what the optimiser found

- **Left panel:** progress of the five overnight searches. x-axis = round (0 = starting drive); y-axis =
  |C_A − C_B| (higher is better). Dark line = best search, grey = the other four. Dashed = no drive; dotted = best
  single tone from figure 2.
  - All five beat no drive; four of five beat the best single tone. Best: 0.0206 (+35%). Several lines still
    rising at the end: not converged.
- **Right panel:** each pair's contrast for no drive, best single tone and the optimised drive (blue classical,
  orange CISS).
  - The optimised drive strengthens the CISS compass (0.0206 → 0.0250) and weakens the classical one
    (0.0054 → 0.0044), which is what widens the gap.
- The best drive is essentially one tone at about 4 MHz. It beats the tone scan partly because the optimiser also
  tunes the phase, which the scan kept at zero.

## Caveats to say out loud

- The overnight run is not converged: 5 rounds, most of which hit the optimiser's step limit.
- The drive strength (0.35 MHz = 0.25 × the Zeeman frequency) is a placeholder.
- No null test (comparison against random drives) yet for the contrast measure.
- The single-tone scan held the phase at zero and stepped 0.5 MHz.
